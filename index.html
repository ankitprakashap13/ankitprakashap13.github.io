Engineering
& leadership

Ankit has spent his career on the surfaces enterprises actually touch — database consoles, payment platforms, incident dashboards. The kind of work that is invisible when it goes right.

  1. 2026 — present

    Senior Software Engineer

    Atlassian · Bangalore

    Ankit works on IT Service Management — the platform enterprises reach for when something breaks. Incidents, changes, service requests, and the status pages the rest of the world watches during an outage.

    “A status page is the calmest surface of the loudest moment. I like that the work is invisible when it goes right.”
    what that looked like
    • Architected GraphQL APIs with Relay entrypoints for Jira Service Management incident management, enabling code-splitting and lazy-loaded features — a markedly lighter initial bundle and faster time-to-interactive.
    • Built AI-powered incident intelligence: LLMs that summarise incident playbooks automatically and suggest the right stakeholders from historical patterns and feature ownership.
    • Designed UI onboarding spotlights with AI-guided context detection, lifting new-user adoption and cutting the support tickets that follow confusion.
    • Raised Jira Service Management to WCAG 2.1 AA accessibility while shipping the AI and GraphQL work — inclusive by default, not retrofitted.
    • Improved status page reliability and incident response processes, sharpening real-time service visibility for enterprise customers.
    • GraphQL
    • Relay
    • React
    • TypeScript
    • LLM agents
    • WCAG 2.1 AA
    • Jira Service Management

  2. 2020 — 2026

    Manager of Software Development

    Oracle · Bangalore

    One of the first developers Oracle hired in India for its Cloud Infrastructure team. He arrived as employee-one on the database console and left running the team that owns it — promoted from Senior Engineer to Manager in December 2023.

    “I joined as the first frontend engineer for the console in India and left managing the team that owns it. That arc is the thing I’m proudest of.”
    what that looked like
    • Foundational developer for the OCI DBaaS UI — he set the frontend standards and built the core modules that shipped globally.
    • Architected the React consoles for Autonomous Database, Oracle Base Database Service, and Exadata Database Service across every deployment model: Dedicated Infrastructure, Exascale Infrastructure, Cloud@Customer, and External Database.
    • Owned Autonomous Database UI end to end, across both the ADBS and ADBD verticals.
    • Shipped the hard functionality — multi-cloud integrations with Azure, AWS and GCP, Pluggable Database management, Data Guard, disaster recovery, and elastic pool scaling — backend and frontend both.
    • Led the UI modernisation to Oracle Redwood across dozens of database service pages and several repositories; other teams adopted the approach.
    • Established the CI/CD framework on Jenkins — zero-downtime deployments and release cycles measured in days rather than weeks.
    • Hired and mentored a team with zero attrition, and coached two engineers to promotion.

track two · the scientist

Data science
& AI/ML

The same eleven years, read through a different lens. Every job I have had was about inferring what someone needed before they finished asking — the models just made that tractable.

  1. 2024 · the turn

    Master of Data Science

    Deakin University · Melbourne

    Eleven years in, Ankit went back to school — not to leave engineering, but because the interesting problems had started moving somewhere his existing tools could not reach.

    “I didn’t want to be the engineer who wires up someone else’s model and hopes. I wanted to know why it works.”

  2. 2026 · Atlassian

    Incident intelligence

    LLMs in production

    An LLM feature that reads incident playbooks and summarises them automatically, then suggests the right stakeholders by learning from historical patterns and feature ownership. It shortens the worst part of an outage: working out who to wake up.

  3. 2026 · Atlassian

    Code review agents

    multi-step prompting & tool orchestration

    Agents wired into pull-request comment analysis that propose review suggestions rather than waiting to be asked. Ankit also authored the SKILL.md documentation framework that standardises how code agents plug into development workflow — the reason adoption spread across engineering teams rather than stalling in one.

  4. 2026 · Atlassian

    Prompt optimisation pipeline

    reliability engineering for language models

    Tuned system prompts and few-shot examples for ITSM-specific tasks and drove the hallucination rate down by most of its original magnitude. Treating prompts as an artefact with a regression suite, rather than a lucky sentence.

  5. 2020 — 2026 · Oracle

    AI-assisted schema analysis

    natural language over databases

    LLM-powered natural language queries that let customers navigate a database schema by describing what they wanted instead of learning where it lived. Support queries fell noticeably; time-to-productivity improved for people who had never opened a console before.

  6. 2020 — 2026 · Oracle

    AI-driven analytics insights

    instrumentation that argues back

    Adobe Analytics wired into an insights engine that automated feature usage and funnel analysis — turning a dashboard nobody read into product decisions the team could defend.

  7. 2018 — 2020 · Razorpay

    Recommendation engine

    collaborative filtering & behavioural clustering

    Bank offer inventories are enormous and almost entirely irrelevant to any one customer. Ankit built collaborative filtering with behaviour-based clustering to fix that — engagement with offers and wallet activation both moved sharply.

  8. 2016 — 2018 · Healthi

    Smart Select

    the first one

    An adaptive questionnaire that inferred the right healthcare package from a handful of answers, converting far better than the static form it replaced. Years before the vocabulary existed, this was already a recommendation problem.

  9. 2014 — 2016 · Thomson Reuters

    Legal document indexing

    data engineering, before the name

    Crawlers and spider scanners processing legal documents at volume and high accuracy. Ankit didn’t call it data engineering at the time. It was.
